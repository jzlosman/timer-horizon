# Design System: Timer Horizon

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Planetary Totalizer"**

Timer Horizon is an after-hours scientific instrument: a full-screen typographic current collapsing toward a luminous central event horizon. The central duration is the single stable, human-scale object. Everything else—abstract glyphs, living figures, and light—moves because the world is larger than the visitor's clock.

The experience is dense but never dashboard-like. Its motion, not ornament, supplies the spectacle. Facts are briefly legible bodies travelling within the field; the background is explicitly abstract and never pretends to be code or telemetry.

**Key Characteristics:**
- A calm duration inside a living accretion ring.
- Soot-dark space, mineral text, and hot stellar light.
- Shader-driven currents and restrained optical distortion.
- No cards, graph furniture, glowing terminal tropes, or decorative controls.

## Colors

Color is physical light: not scattered accent paint. The dark field stays nearly neutral; the ring supplies the heat.

**The One-Star Rule.** The luminous ring is the only major saturated form. Facts may borrow its pale heat, but nothing else competes with it.

## Typography

The timer reads like human language, not a stopwatch. Facts and glyphs use an unadorned, compact technical face with tabular figures; the central duration is generous, calm, and high contrast.

**The Human-Clock Rule.** The duration always says `5 minutes, 12 seconds`, never a cold digit-only chronometer.

## Layout

The viewport is the composition. The central timer owns a protected circular void; active facts travel on legible paths around it, while microglyph currents cross the remaining field. Mobile preserves the singularity and reduces particle density rather than turning the page into cards.

## Elevation & Depth

There are no panels or shadows. Depth comes from particle parallax, velocity, fade, and the ring's soft bloom. The timer is crisp HTML above the field and is never distorted by the shader.

## Shapes

No rounded containers. The event horizon is the recurring form: a broken, elliptical ring with a bright inner edge and an irregular dust band.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** make motion show gravitational pull: streams bend, shear, accelerate, and disappear.
- **Do** keep real facts readable, linked, and visibly distinct from the abstract field.
- **Do** let empty space feel interactive without adding a visible button.

### Don't:
- **Don't** render fake code, terminal windows, charts, HUD brackets, or a literal black-hole illustration.
- **Don't** use rainbow color, green-on-black hacker styling, or a generic neon glow.
- **Don't** warp or animate the central timer away from legibility.
